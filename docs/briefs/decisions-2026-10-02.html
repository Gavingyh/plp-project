The decisions

1. Aspect method: switch to the LLM classifier? Team decision by 5 Oct

The database assigns aspects by similarity to seed-term centroids. A local LLM (llama3.1:8b) picking one aspect per sentence is built and measured. Its corpus run is complete: all 44,345 sentences, 3 Oct.

optionwhat the evidence says
Keep centroidsMatches the human aspect 41% of the time (always guessing the commonest: 37%); 6.4 aspects a sentence; every named aspect of every bank comes out positive, because each inherits the bank's overall tone.
LLM, llama alone73% against the human aspects (macro-F1 0.68); one aspect a sentence; already run on the whole corpus, so it costs nothing more. On the full corpus the headline is unchanged and the aspects separate: retail banking (43% of sentences) turns slightly negative for all three banks; DBS asset quality falls from +0.27 to +0.07.
LLM, two models, disagreements to other84% where llama and qwen agree (76% of sentences); needs a second corpus run (most of a day); a quarter of sentences lose their aspect, and where the two disagree llama is right 40% of the time, qwen 36%.

Recommendation: llama alone. It nearly doubles accuracy, needs no second run, and keeps every sentence's aspect. Report the two-model agreement on the labelled set as the confidence evidence, not as a rule on the corpus.

If not decided by the date: the re-score week (12–18 Oct) runs on centroid aspects, and the per-aspect results in the report rest on the 41% method.

optionwhat the evidence says

3. One blended index, or each source side by side? Team decision by 5 Oct

Earnings text is far more positive than Reddit for every bank, so a blended score tracks each bank's source mix as much as its sentiment.

optionwhat the evidence says
Blended headline onlyDBS +0.122, OCBC +0.136, UOB +0.074: UOB looks worst only because it publishes less earnings text.
Per source onlyReddit: DBS −0.003, OCBC −0.029, UOB −0.022 (no real difference). Earnings: DBS +0.286, OCBC +0.375, UOB +0.406. Google News in between.
BothThe blend as the headline the proposal promised, with the per-source rows beside it; the API and dashboard already have them.

Recommendation: both, with the per-source rows shown next to the headline and the report explaining the source-mix effect.

If not decided by the date: the dashboard and report show the blend alone, and an examiner can fairly say UOB's low score is an artefact.

4. Is a bank economist's forecast "about the bank"? People by 5 Oct

Sentences such as "UOB revises up Vietnam's 2026 GDP growth forecast to 8.5%" name the bank as the source of a macro view. The guideline's first step marks text not about the bank and labels nothing else.

optionwhat the evidence says
Not about the bankAs the guideline reads now. Opus marked 25 of the 52 top-up macro rows this way; macro would end near 49 labelled rows, short of 80.
About the bank, macro aspectMacro would pass 80. But the sentiment of a GDP forecast isn't sentiment toward the bank, so it would dilute what the macro score means.

Recommendation: not about the bank. Accept macro as a thin aspect and say so, rather than redefine sentiment to fill a quota.

If not decided by the date: annotators decide case by case and the top-up labels become inconsistent.

5. Wealth or retail banking for priority-banking products? People by 5 Oct

DBS Treasures, OCBC Premier, Bank of Singapore and unit trusts bought at a bank: the boundary behind 63 of Opus's aspect disagreements and the classifier's weakest aspect (wealth F1 0.50).

optionwhat the evidence says
By productInvestment products and priority or private banking → wealth; deposit accounts, cards, loans, fees, the app → retail, whoever the customer is. Already close to the classifier's wording, which doesn't name unit trusts: adding them is an edit to test on the labelled set (under an hour) before any redo.
By customerAnything for an affluent customer → wealth. Simpler to state, harder to apply from one sentence. Changes the classifier's definitions, so the corpus run is redone: about a day of the MacBook.

Recommendation: by product, written into the annotation guideline's table before the top-up round.

If not decided by the date: the top-up labels repeat the inconsistency the Opus round found. Changing only the labelling guideline needs no redo, but the classifier is then scored against labels that follow a different rule on this boundary.

6. DBS Vickers: the bank, or a separate broker? Team decision by 12 Oct

456 sentences change attribution on this rule alone, about three fifths of everything NER changes. Who owns it is settled: DBS Vickers Securities (Singapore) is in DBS Group's own list of subsidiaries (31 Dec 2024); UOB Kay Hian is separately listed on SGX, with UOB holding about a third.

optionwhat the evidence says
A broker (current rule)Rests on the plan's "brokers, not the bank's own business", which is true of UOB Kay Hian but not of DBS Vickers. Removes 456 sentences from DBS.
DBSThe rule already applied to POSB, PayLah! and Great Eastern: subsidiaries count as the parent. 451 of the 456 are Reddit customers on fees, paying upfront, CDP and the app, a DBS service; annotators treated its trades as banking. None is DBS research about another bank.

Recommendation: count DBS Vickers as DBS, and keep UOB Kay Hian out; with ownership settled, the team only needs to confirm. The index measures sentiment toward the bank as people experience it. One line: the vickers entry in preprocess/bank_ner.py.

If not decided by the date: the current rule stands.

7. Scoring model: fine-tuned FinBERT or base FinBERT? Team decision by 12 Oct

Every stored sentiment score comes from base FinBERT, used zero-shot; the fine-tuned model is the DNN the rubric requires. The 1 Oct seeds were trained from a relabelled head (a bug, fixed 3 Oct) and are no longer an option, but scripts/rescore.py still defaults to them (DEFAULT_FINETUNED in config/model_version.py): adopting the retrained seeds needs a new entry there.

optionwhat the evidence says
Base FinBERTTest macro-F1 0.571, accuracy 0.70. Calls most sentences neutral; finds few of the negatives.
Fine-tuned, retrained 4 Oct, averagedEvery seed beats base: test macro-F1 0.646, 0.592, 0.625 (mean 0.621 ± 0.027). The average scores 0.634 (accuracy 0.702) without a margin and 0.612 (accuracy 0.702) with the margin chosen on dev, 0.10. That margin is a single-model stand-in: no clean dev set exists for the three-seed average, 320 of the 380 out-of-fold dev rows are scored by one seed, and averaging flattens the probabilities, so the average's own best margin is likely smaller. On test the margin makes no measurable difference (paired bootstrap: −0.022, 95% interval −0.058 to +0.009). On the full corpus (previewed at margin 0.12, not 0.10) the average stays within 0.015 of base FinBERT's headlines (DBS +0.132, OCBC +0.122, UOB +0.084; DBS just ahead of OCBC, under every seed) and agrees with base on every aspect's sign; 62% of sentences come out neutral. Seed 42 alone still raises every headline (DBS +0.171), and the seeds disagree in sign on 5 of 30 aspect cells.

Recommendation: the retrained average, on the stated criterion that the index exists to surface positive and negative sentiment (macro-F1): the 1 Oct seeds carry a known bug. Keep the margin as chosen on dev (0.10) and report the argmax score beside it: on test the margin makes no measurable difference, and dropping it because of the test result would be choosing on the test split. Report base FinBERT's ranking and the per-seed range beside the headlines, and call the DBS–OCBC order unsettled. Adopting the retrained models changes the reported fine-tuning result (0.604 ± 0.035 to 0.621 ± 0.027) everywhere it appears. Either way, applying it is quick: scripts/rescore.py, tested on a copy on 3 Oct, applies both changes in seconds from the saved probabilities; the headlines at 0.10 haven't been previewed and will differ slightly from the 0.12 figures above.

If not decided by the date: the DNN stays an offline result, and the system the report describes doesn't use it.

8. Chatbot settings: strip invented evidence; rank evidence by the aspect's name Team decision by 12 Oct

Both are built and off by default.

optionwhat the evidence says
CHATBOT_ENFORCE_EVIDENCE=stripReplayed on 3 Oct with stricter guards (quoted text, signs and percentages now checked): citation warnings 11 → 0 on the 8B and 16 → 0 on the 3B; answers with a figure no tool returned 10 → 0 and 12 → 2, both inventions the check now catches. Live on 2 Oct, tool choice unchanged. Answers that didn't search show no quotes and say why.
CHATBOT_ASPECT_QUERY=1Offline MRR 0.559 against 0.217 for question-phrased queries. Live, evidence is more on topic but often report boilerplate.

Recommendation: switch both on. Strip has no measured downside; the aspect query is better than chance-level retrieval even with the boilerplate.

If not decided by the date: the demo runs with invented citations flagged but shown.

13. Reddit and CNA text to the paid hosted backends? Team decision by 12 Oct

Since 4 Oct the Gemini backend never receives Reddit text, CNA text or text from an unlisted source (config/sources.yaml). The Claude and OpenAI backends still do.

Recommendation: apply the same rule to every hosted backend unless CNA's terms allow sending its text to an outside service; Reddit text is other people's posts either way.

If not decided by the date: only Gemini withholds them.

14. Count POSB as DBS at collection? Team decision by 12 Oct

The news filter and the bank tagging count POSB as DBS; the aliases used at collection (ingest/base.py) don't. A Reddit post naming only POSB is never stored (ingest/arctic_shift.py:139-140), and a sentence naming only POSB is dropped at segmentation (preprocess/pipeline.py:66-73).

Recommendation: add it. It affects future collection only: the posts and sentences already missed come back only if collection is run again for those periods.

If not decided by the date: POSB-only posts and sentences stay out of the corpus.
