By Monday 5 October

The aspect decision starts the critical path; the rest of this week's items are quick or unblock labelling.

  • Run the LLM aspect classifier on the whole corpus, and choose the disagreement rule Decision by 5 Oct

    Why: the database's aspects come from seed centroids, which give a sentence about 6.4 aspects and match the human's aspect only 41% of the time; llama3.1:8b matches 73%, and 84% where qwen3:4b agrees. On a 1,000-sentence preview, switching turns several aspects from positive to negative (DBS asset quality +0.26 → −0.26), so every per-aspect claim in the report depends on this. The rule for the 24% of sentences where the two models disagree (keep llama's answer, or mark them other) has to be chosen with it.

    Unblocks: the corpus run (about 24 h; resumable, and the answer cache already started filling overnight), then the re-score. Source: llm-aspect-classifier, index-preview.
  • O1: confirm the aspect approach with the supervisor Decision by 5 Oct

    Why: it keeps the proposal's aspects but changes how sentences are assigned to them (an LLM instead of centroids) and the taxonomy grew from four aspects to ten. A change from the proposal that the supervisor hasn't seen is a marking risk; the report must explain it either way.

    Source: PLAN, decision O1; DESIGN 21.
  • O2: one blended index, or each source's index side by side Decision by 5 Oct

    Why: UOB has the highest earnings-only score and the lowest blended one, only because it publishes less earnings text than DBS. The headline ranking the report and the dashboard show depends on this choice, so it has to be made before results are computed.

    Source: PLAN, decision O2.
  • Settle wealth versus retail banking for investment products bought at a bank Decision by 5 Oct

    Why: it is the largest single cause of disagreement between our aspect labels and Opus's (63 rows), and the classifier's weakest boundary. The answer has to be in the annotation guideline before the top-up round, or the new labels repeat the inconsistency.

    Unblocks: the top-up labelling. Source: opus-labeller.
  • Fix the dashboard's analyst-consensus panel Dashboard owner by 5 Oct

    Why: the panel takes one "latest date" for all three banks, so UOB's consensus, which has an older date, doesn't show. The API fixed the same bug on 1 Oct; the dashboard reads the database directly and still has it. It is a one-line change: use ingest.yahoo_analyst.latest_per_bank(conn).

    Source: DESIGN 23 (amendment), examiner assessment §1.3.
  • Get a free Groq API key for larger chatbot models People this week

    Why: the code is ready to use three far larger hosted models (gpt-oss-120b, llama-3.3-70b, qwen3.8-27b) with no other change, which gives the demo-model decision a hosted option and verifies the OpenAI-compatible backend (O4). Sign-up needs a person; switch on Zero Data Retention, since evidence searches send Reddit text. Setup: RUNBOOK, "Chatbot backends".

    Unblocks: the three-model evaluation (Claude, about 45 min each). Optional: an NVIDIA NIM key works the same way.
  • Review the Opus disagreements on the test split first (80 rows) People start this week

    Why: Opus disagreed with our labels on 202 aspects and 173 sentiments. The 80 on the test split (43 aspect, 37 sentiment) decide whether test labels need correcting before the models are re-evaluated; the rest feed the guideline. Fill the decision column in data/labeled/claude/review_*.csv.

    Source: opus-labeller.

By Monday 12 October

Labels and the remaining model decisions, so the re-score week can run once.

  • Label the top-up sheet: 268 rows, the first 40 by two people People 5–11 Oct

    Why: five aspects have fewer than 80 labelled rows (macro 22, dividends 33, capital and liquidity 49, NIM 58, asset quality 78), too few to evaluate the classifier or the model on them. The 40 shared rows give the first human–human aspect agreement figure the project has; today there is only human–LLM. The sheet is blind on purpose. NIM may land just above 80, because many of its Reddit rows are really about customer rates.

    File: data/labeled/topup/topup_sheet.csv; instructions in its README. Then: Claude merges the labels (a merge script is still to write). Source: labelling-topup.
  • Label the bank-NER gold set (201 sentences) People 5–11 Oct

    Why: NER is the project's third text-mining task, but it has no accuracy figure until these are labelled, so it can't yet be claimed in the report. The set over-samples the hard cases (brokers, product names, tickers).

    File: data/labeled/ner/gold_sheet.csv. Unblocks: NER evaluation (Claude, under an hour). Source: bank-ner.
  • Decide what "DBS Vickers" means: the bank, or a separate broker Decision by 12 Oct

    Why: 456 sentences change attribution on this one rule, about three fifths of everything NER changes. It defines what "DBS" means in the index, and it is needed before NER can be applied or the gold set scored.

    Source: bank-ner, examiner assessment (1 Oct) Q9.
  • Decide the scoring model: the fine-tuned models, or base FinBERT Decision by 12 Oct

    Why: the stored scores are base FinBERT's, so the DNN the rubric requires doesn't yet affect what the product shows. The measured best is the three fine-tuned seeds averaged with a dev-chosen neutral margin (test macro-F1 0.624, accuracy 0.682, against 0.571 and 0.70). Switching raises every headline by 0.06–0.19 and puts UOB ahead of DBS, so the report's conclusions follow this choice. State the criterion first. For the report: on 2 Oct, cross-validation on two more seeds showed the readable-label input chosen on 1 Oct is no better than the aspect key, so credit no single change for the gain over 30 Sep; the models themselves stand.

    Source: finetune-fixes, error-analysis, index-preview, noise-floor-cv.
  • Decide whether evidence search ranks by the aspect's name (CHATBOT_ASPECT_QUERY) Decision by 12 Oct

    Why: with the question as the query, search ranks no better than random (MRR 0.217, random 0.217); with the aspect's name, 0.559. On 1 Oct a live answer presented card-fee and fraud complaints as "complaints about UOB's asset quality", and every automated check passed it. Claude re-runs the gold set with the flag on first, so the decision rests on live answers.

    Source: chatbot-eval-build (retrieval; Live runs, evidence-2).
  • O5: drop passing mentions ("paid via DBS PayNow")? Decision by 12 Oct

    Why: it changes which sentences are in the data, so it has to be decided before the one final re-score, not after.

    Source: PLAN, decision O5.

    Why: on 1 Oct, 25 confirmed invented citations (11 of 32 answers on the 8B model, 14 on the 3B) all came from answers that never searched for evidence. The harness knows when no search was made, so it can drop the section, or run the search itself and print real quotes, instead of appending a warning.
