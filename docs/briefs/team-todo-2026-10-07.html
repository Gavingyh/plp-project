1. Still to decide

The owner settled the three open points on 11 Oct (blended index, local-context questions, data repairs). What is left here are follow-ups that need the team’s word before code or rules change.

  • Approve two fixes to bank recognition that the local-context research found Decision before the re-score

    Why: bank recognition decides which bank a sentence counts for. Tried on made-up sentences: “Development Bank of Singapore”, DBS’s name until 2003, resolves to OCBC; and a sentence naming only PayLah, digibank or TMRW resolves to no bank, although each is one bank’s product. Both are small code changes in preprocess/bank_ner.py with tests, and they change which sentences each bank gets, so they belong in the one re-score.

    Source: docs/local-context-questions.md, answers of 11 Oct.
  • Adopt six of the seven recommended answers; drop the sarcasm flag Decision before retraining (12 Oct)

    Why: the seven judgment calls among the researched answers were checked on 11 Oct against the labelled rows (test rows left out), the whole corpus and their sources. Six hold: a bank economist’s forecast is not about the bank; a scam the bank did not cause is not its outage; a regular monthly share purchase is neutral; “lol” does not change the label; a “buy” beside a tiny upside is not a bullish view; report boilerplate stays out of the evidence. Opus’s labels already follow the first two, so adopting them changes no training label. The seventh, a sarcasm flag for annotators, is not worth adding: 7 marked sentences in 45,844 and none in the labelled rows. Two things to do with them: decide what happens to the 134 rows Opus flags as not about the bank, which are still trained on; and filter boilerplate at the chatbot’s evidence search.

    Evidence: docs/evidence/local-context-validation-2026-10-11.md. Verdicts are also under each answer in docs/local-context-questions.md.
  • Which aspects the dashboard and chatbot show: the LLM’s, unless the team says otherwise Default set applied in the re-score

    Why: the team decided on 10 Oct to run and report both methods. The index, the dashboard and the chatbot can read only one set of aspects, so the re-score will apply the LLM’s, which match people’s aspect 73% of the time against the centroids’ 41% (84% where the two local models agree); the report gives both. Where the two local models disagree (24% of sentences), llama’s answer is kept. Say so before the re-score if the team wants the centroids shown instead.

    Source: llm-aspect-classifier, index-preview evidence.

2. Labels: Opus 5.5 labels every sheet (decided 10 October)

Done on 10 Oct. People no longer label the sentiment sheets. The labels are recorded and reported as LLM labels, not human labels: the meeting note said to declare them as human, and the owner changed that on 10 Oct, because the report has to declare its GenAI use and the labels must be described as what they are.

  • Opus 5.5 labelled every sheet under the new rules: 2,075 rows Done 10 Oct done

    Result: the first round’s 1,277 rows, the top-up sheet’s 268, the active-learning sheet’s 150 and the broker research round’s 380, each labelled for aspect and then sentiment by Opus raters that saw no other label (36 runs, audited). 757 positive, 879 neutral, 439 negative; 134 flagged as not about the bank. Against people on the 1,277 rows: aspect κ 0.802, sentiment κ 0.793, the same as the 1 Oct run under the old rules. The labels are in their own folder and have replaced nothing; the first item in section 1 decides how they are used.

    Files: data/labeled/llm_rater/rules-2026-10-10/. Evidence: docs/evidence/llm-rater-new-rules-2026-10-10.md.
  • Read the two new rules as written into the guideline People before retraining (12 Oct)

    Why: the rules were decided on 10 Oct; this is only a check of how they were worded, since Opus labelled 2,075 rows from this wording. (1) Retail products: a feature clearly good for the customer is positive, one clearly bad is negative; a bare mention, a question, a how-to or mixed terms stays neutral. (2) Wealth: the priority and private tiers and the wealth segment’s figures; loans, mortgages and unit trusts are retail unless the sentence is about a wealth tier. The instructions also sent brokerage accounts, regular savings plans, insurance and robo-advisers to retail, which the team’s note did not say. If a wording is wrong, the affected rows get another pass.

    File: docs/annotation-guideline.md, “Two rules decided on 2026-10-10”.

    Why: NER is the project’s third text-mining task, and it has no human accuracy figure until these are labelled. The 10 Oct decision named the sentiment sheets, not this one; Opus has already labelled it blind as a reference (2 Oct), so the team can also decide to use that and report it as an LLM reference.

    File: data/labeled/ner/gold_sheet.csv. Source: bank-ner.

On hold, or nearly done

  • Chatbot speed: free the Mac’s memory before a demo; one option left to check Mostly done 10 Oct before the demo

    Status: measured on 10 Oct, the local model’s speed halved within three minutes as the Mac ran short of memory (11.4 tokens a second down to 5.2, with other programs open), so five questions took 25 to 105 seconds. That is the main cause of slow answers, and closing heavy programs before a demo is the fix. One option is built and left off: writing the tool descriptions where Ollama caches them, instead of having the model read all 967 tokens again for every question (about 10 seconds). On the gold set it was no faster on average (median 36.8 against 41.3 seconds, mean 42.9 against 42.3) and no better on the checks (right tool 22 of 24 against 23), in a run disturbed by other work; rerun it on a quiet Mac before turning it on.

    Evidence: docs/evidence/chatbot-timeout-and-speed-2026-10-10.md. Setting: OLLAMA_TOOLS_IN_SYSTEM.
