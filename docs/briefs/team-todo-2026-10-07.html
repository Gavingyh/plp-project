1. Decide first: the upstream items

Every later label, model and score depends on these, so settling them later means repeating work. Claude has drafted material for the first three; each draft proposes, the team decides.

  • The aspect method: the LLM’s aspects or the centroids, and the disagreement rule Decision overdue (5 Oct)

    Why: the database’s aspects come from seed centroids, which give a sentence about 6.4 aspects and match the human’s aspect 41% of the time; llama3.1:8b matches 73%, and 84% where qwen3:4b agrees. Under centroids every aspect of every bank is positive; with the LLM, retail banking turns slightly negative for all three banks and DBS asset quality falls from +0.27 to +0.07. Every per-aspect claim in the report depends on this. Choose with it the rule for the 24% of sentences where the two models disagree: keep llama’s answer, or mark them other. The corpus run is done (3 Oct); changing an aspect’s definition or examples means redoing it, about a day of the MacBook, so test a new wording first on the 1,277 labelled sentences (under an hour).

    Source: llm-aspect-classifier, index-preview evidence. Decision 5 in the decision brief.
  • Does a product’s feature count as sentiment? Decision before any labelling or retraining

    Why: the fine-tuned model’s commonest error is a neutral sentence called positive or negative, most of the retail ones describing a product’s feature (“I use OCBC Frank 6% for daily expense”, called positive), and every one of Opus’s 173 sentiment disagreements with the annotators is on where neutral ends. Draft ready: three candidate rules with train/dev examples and how many rows each would change. “Customer value” fits the current labels (about 4 rows change), though those labels may follow the suggestion model; “changes only” measures opinion more strictly (about 30 rows change).

    Draft: docs/briefs/proposal-product-features-2026-10-07.md. Unblocks the top-up and active-learning labels.
  • Wealth or retail for priority-banking products, and the 22 open local-context questions Decision overdue (5 Oct)

    Why: DBS Treasures, OCBC Premier and bank-sold unit trusts are the boundary behind 63 of Opus’s aspect disagreements, and wealth is one of the classifier’s weakest aspects. The other questions (savings-rate cuts, bank economists, slang, questions as sentiment) each change labels or rules. Draft ready: a proposed answer to each, with evidence, confidence and what changes if the team answers otherwise; three terms need a local reader (“xmm”, “Lobang Sis”, “FHR”). Write the team’s answers into docs/local-context-questions.md.

    Draft: docs/briefs/local-context-proposals-2026-10-07.md. Decision 5 in the decision brief. A “by customer” answer changes the aspect definitions, so the corpus run is redone (about a day).
  • Relabel blind: the 299 test rows, and a sample of training rows? Decision overdue (5 Oct)

    Why: annotators saw a model’s suggestion while labelling. The test labels match the suggestion on 89% of rows, and the training rows kept its sentiment on 904 of 978 in the first round (92%) and 263 of 280 in the broker research round (94%). Every test score inherits that anchoring, the first threat to validity an examiner will raise. A blind relabel of the test rows, and of about 100 training rows to measure the effect, has to happen before the 12–18 Oct re-evaluation to count.

    Source: annotation-agreement (27 Sep); data/labeled/candidates.csv label against llm_prelabel; broker research round files.
  • One blended index, or each source’s index side by side Decision overdue (5 Oct)

    Why: UOB has the highest earnings-only score and the lowest blended one, only because it publishes less earnings text than DBS. The ranking the report and the dashboard show depends on this choice, and broker research adds another source to weigh.

    Source: PLAN, blended-index decision.

3. Decide by Monday 12 October

  • The scoring model: base FinBERT, or one of three fine-tuned sets Decision by 12 Oct

    Why: the stored scores are base FinBERT’s, so the DNN the rubric requires doesn’t yet affect what the product shows. Three candidates: the 1 Oct models (0.604 ± 0.035 test macro-F1 against base 0.571, the figures the claim uses), the 4 Oct retrain (0.621 ± 0.027), and the adapted models (FinBERT further trained on the corpus’s own sentences first: on cross-validation 0.701 against 0.663, better in 13 of 15 paired folds; on test 0.628 ± 0.022, within the seed noise). The adapted models must be pretrained again before adoption: a 7 Oct validation found their pretraining text held 151 sentences related to test sentences (a filter gap, fixed that day). State the criterion first; whichever is chosen is retrained on the final labels in the 12–18 Oct week.

    Source: retrain (4 Oct), dapt (6–7 Oct), sgx-research-evaluation evidence. Decision 7 in the decision brief.
