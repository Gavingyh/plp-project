1. Still to decide: what the 10 October decisions leave open

The first two come straight out of the 10 Oct decisions and block the retraining. The rest were open before.

  • Which labels the models train and test on, now that the retail rule has changed Decision before retraining (12 Oct)

    Why: two of the 10 Oct decisions pull apart. Retail products now carry sentiment directed at the bank, so rows labelled neutral under the old rule change. The test rows are not relabelled, so the 299 test labels stay on the old rule. If the train/dev rows follow the new rule and the test rows the old one, test scores fall for a reason that has nothing to do with the model. Choose one: apply the new rule to the retail test rows too (Opus’s labels, reported as LLM labels), or keep them and say so in the report. Decide with it, for the 1,277 rows people already labelled, whether the training label is the person’s or Opus’s new one. What the 10 Oct run shows: on the 299 test rows Opus differs from people on 47 aspects and 42 sentiments, and on the 978 train/dev rows on 155 and 127; the retail rule itself moved 34 of 280 neutral retail rows to positive or negative, about as many as change between two Opus runs.

    Decisions of 10 Oct on retail products, the test rows and Opus labelling. Until this is settled, Opus’s new labels sit beside the human ones in data/labeled/claude/ and change nothing.
  • Which aspect method the product serves, and the disagreement rule Decision before the re-score

    Why: the team decided on 10 Oct to run both methods, the centroids and the LLM, and the report will give both. The index, the dashboard and the chatbot still read one set of aspects, so one has to be the served one. The centroids give a sentence about 6.4 aspects and match the human’s aspect 41% of the time; llama3.1:8b matches 73%, and 84% where qwen3:4b agrees. Under centroids every aspect of every bank is positive; with the LLM, retail banking turns slightly negative for all three banks and DBS asset quality falls from +0.27 to +0.07. Choose with it the rule for the 24% of sentences where the two models disagree: keep llama’s answer, or mark them other.

    Source: llm-aspect-classifier, index-preview evidence. Decision 5 in the decision brief.
  • The 22 open local-context questions Decision overdue (5 Oct)

    Why: wealth or retail is settled (10 Oct). The others (savings-rate cuts, bank economists, slang, questions as sentiment) each change labels or rules, and Opus labels under whatever the guideline says on the day. Draft ready: a proposed answer to each, with evidence, confidence and what changes if the team answers otherwise; three terms need a local reader (“xmm”, “Lobang Sis”, “FHR”). Write the team’s answers into docs/local-context-questions.md.

    Draft: docs/briefs/local-context-proposals-2026-10-07.md.
  • One blended index, or each source’s index side by side Decision overdue (5 Oct)

    Why: UOB has the highest earnings-only score and the lowest blended one, only because it publishes less earnings text than DBS. The ranking the report and the dashboard show depends on this choice, and broker research adds another source to weigh.

    Source: PLAN, blended-index decision.
  • Change the project rule that Opus labels never replace the human labels Owner before Opus’s labels train a model

    Why: CLAUDE.md and DESIGN decision 22 both say Opus is an additional rater whose labels never replace a person’s. The 10 Oct labelling decision reverses that for the sheets nobody has labelled, and possibly for the 1,277 rows (first item above). The rule and the decision log should say what the team now means, so later sessions follow it.

    CLAUDE.md, Rules; docs/DESIGN.md, decision 22.

2. Labels: Opus 5.5 labels every sheet (decided 10 October)

Done on 10 Oct. People no longer label the sentiment sheets. The labels are recorded and reported as LLM labels, not human labels: the meeting note said to declare them as human, and the owner changed that on 10 Oct, because the report has to declare its GenAI use and the labels must be described as what they are.

  • Opus 5.5 labelled every sheet under the new rules: 2,075 rows Done 10 Oct done

    Result: the first round’s 1,277 rows, the top-up sheet’s 268, the active-learning sheet’s 150 and the broker research round’s 380, each labelled for aspect and then sentiment by Opus raters that saw no other label (36 runs, audited). 757 positive, 879 neutral, 439 negative; 134 flagged as not about the bank. Against people on the 1,277 rows: aspect κ 0.802, sentiment κ 0.793, the same as the 1 Oct run under the old rules. The labels are in their own folder and have replaced nothing; the first item in section 1 decides how they are used.

    Files: data/labeled/claude/rules-2026-10-10/. Evidence: docs/evidence/opus-labels-new-rules-2026-10-10.md.
  • Confirm the wording of the two new rules, and whether to redo the corpus aspect run Decision before retraining (12 Oct)

    Why: the rules are now in the guideline and were Opus’s instructions, worded from the one-line decisions. (1) Retail products: a feature clearly good for the customer is positive, one clearly bad is negative; a bare mention, a question, a how-to or mixed terms stays neutral (the “customer value” option of the 7 Oct paper). (2) Wealth: the priority and private tiers and the wealth segment’s figures; loans, mortgages and unit trusts are retail unless the sentence is about a wealth tier. The instructions also sent brokerage accounts, regular savings plans, insurance and robo-advisers to retail, which the team’s note did not say. If either wording is wrong, the affected rows need another pass. Separately, the local aspect classifier still uses the old wealth definition: changing it in config/aspects.yaml starts a new cache file, so the corpus aspect run is redone (about a day of the MacBook).

    Files: docs/annotation-guideline.md (“Two rules decided on 2026-10-10”), config/aspects.yaml.
  • Label the bank-NER gold set (201 sentences), or say Opus does this too People by 12 Oct

    Why: NER is the project’s third text-mining task, and it has no human accuracy figure until these are labelled. The 10 Oct decision named the sentiment sheets, not this one; Opus has already labelled it blind as a reference (2 Oct), so the team can also decide to use that and report it as an LLM reference.

    File: data/labeled/ner/gold_sheet.csv. Source: bank-ner.
  • Broker research labels: name the test-row annotator People by 12 Oct

    Why: the 100 blind test rows are labelled but their annotator column is blank (they agree with the LLM’s own labels on 77 of 100, so not copied); naming the annotator lets the report say people labelled them. train.source_round merge refuses rows without an annotator.

    Files: data/labeled/sgx_research/test_blind.csv, candidates.csv.
  • Upload the newer database to the team folder People before the re-score

    Why: a teammate reports a database with newer data, but the plp.db in the team folder is still the 30 Sep one: downloaded twice on 10 Oct, both copies were byte-identical to the 30 Sep snapshot (SHA-256 7ea0da08…5734fb; 28,002 documents, 44,345 sentences, newest document 25 Sep, no broker research rows). The database runs in write-ahead mode, so new rows can sit in plp.db-wal and be missing from an uploaded plp.db: stop the API and dashboard, run sqlite3 data/plp.db "PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE);", then upload the file under a dated name with its SHA-256, so the two can be compared and one made final.

    Compare with shasum -a 256 data/plp.db.
  • Send the broker research source’s database rows from the Windows PC People before the re-score

    Why: the labels point at sentence ids from that PC’s database; rebuilt from the PDFs on the Mac, 52 of the 380 labelled sentences came out with slightly different text and so different ids. On the PC: git pull, then python scripts/source_rows.py export --source sgx_research --out data/run/sgx_rows.db, and send the file (about 2 MB, not through git).

    Source: HANDOFF, 7 October.

On hold, or nearly done

  • Chatbot speed: free the Mac’s memory before a demo; one option left to check Mostly done 10 Oct before the demo

    Status: measured on 10 Oct, the local model’s speed halved within three minutes as the Mac ran short of memory (11.4 tokens a second down to 5.2, with other programs open), so five questions took 25 to 105 seconds. That is the main cause of slow answers, and closing heavy programs before a demo is the fix. One option is built and left off: writing the tool descriptions where Ollama caches them, instead of having the model read all 967 tokens again for every question (about 10 seconds). On the gold set it was no faster on average (median 36.8 against 41.3 seconds, mean 42.9 against 42.3) and no better on the checks (right tool 22 of 24 against 23), in a run disturbed by other work; rerun it on a quiet Mac before turning it on.

    Evidence: docs/evidence/chatbot-timeout-and-speed-2026-10-10.md. Setting: OLLAMA_TOOLS_IN_SYSTEM.
